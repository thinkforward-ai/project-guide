# Starter Role Catalogue

Projects copy only the roles they need into the Roles subsection of the overview's Collaboration section and adapt them there. A project's roster may also define new roles or redefine these; the roster's definition wins. When a project needs expertise that no role here covers, as in a domain new to the framework, define the role from scratch in the roster with the same fields.

A role acts only within the project whose roster lists it. In any other project it may only raise an issue, such as a feature request or a question, naming its origin; it never changes that project's structure, works its tasks, or makes its decisions.

Each role states: **Interest** (what it represents), **Expertise**, **Triggers** (the kinds of decisions it joins), **Red lines**, and **Known bias**.

1. **Stakeholder** (held by the user; never played by the AI)
   1. **Interest:** the project's purpose and its value to them.
   2. **Expertise:** the needs, preferences, and context of the domain.
   3. **Triggers:** reserved decisions, requirements, acceptance, and milestone confirmation.
   4. **Red lines:** set by the stakeholder.
   5. **Known bias:** may favour vision over feasibility.
2. **Project manager**
   1. **Interest:** delivering value within scope, time, and effort, in the right order.
   2. **Expertise:** planning, scope, priorities, risk, and milestones.
   3. **Triggers:** scope, priorities, milestones, effort, sequencing, and process changes. Stays out of technical and design choices.
   4. **Red lines:** unplanned scope growth; work that serves no milestone.
   5. **Known bias:** fast and cheap, at the cost of quality or security.
3. **Domain designer** (for example a game, product, or service designer)
   1. **Interest:** the quality and coherence of the product's core idea.
   2. **Expertise:** design in the project's domain.
   3. **Triggers:** rules, features, content, and changes to the concept.
   4. **Red lines:** features that contradict the concept.
   5. **Known bias:** richer systems and scope growth.
4. **User advocate**
   1. **Interest:** what users see, understand, and feel.
   2. **Expertise:** usability, readability, accessibility, and presentation.
   3. **Triggers:** anything shown to users, and demos.
   4. **Red lines:** confusing or inaccessible presentation.
   5. **Known bias:** hand-holding and over-explanation.
5. **Architect / tech lead**
   1. **Interest:** a sound structure that is easy to change, and a good fit of technology.
   2. **Expertise:** architecture, technology choices, and non-functional requirements.
   3. **Triggers:** technical design, technology, structure, and quality attributes.
   4. **Red lines:** technology lock-in without a decision; designs that cannot be verified.
   5. **Known bias:** over-engineering.
6. **Developer**
   1. **Interest:** a working, verified implementation.
   2. **Expertise:** implementation, testing, and tooling.
   3. **Triggers:** implementation tasks, estimates, and verification.
   4. **Red lines:** unverified changes; tasks with open design choices.
   5. **Known bias:** shortcuts and local fixes.
7. **Guardian** (for example a concept guardian or a framework guardian)
   1. **Interest:** the project's durable definition stays consistent and true to its original goal and purpose.
   2. **Expertise:** the project's goal, its accepted decisions, and its durable definitions.
   3. **Triggers:** any change to the durable definition, such as the concept, the framework, the glossary, or the layout. Its expertise is required on such issues, and its objection blocks the change until it is resolved or the stakeholder overrides it.
   4. **Red lines:** an unresolved contradiction with an accepted decision; the same rule worded differently in different places; additions that do not serve the goal.
   5. **Known bias:** conservatism, resisting useful change.
