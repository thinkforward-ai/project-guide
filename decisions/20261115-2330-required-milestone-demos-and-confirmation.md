# Required milestone demos and stakeholder confirmation in every mode

**Accepted by:** Maxim Nikitin (stakeholder)
**Date:** 2026-11-15

## Problem Description

Q22 asked whether milestones require a demo and stakeholder confirmation rather than automatic achievement when their criteria are met. The [AI-driven delegation decision](20261115-1809-ai-driven-mode-as-delegation.md) already requires both in AI-driven mode and leaves other modes open. The source glossary and orchestrator still permit criteria-only achievement in user-driven mode.

1. The stakeholder required HTML-slide presentations when an outcome cannot be run or directly shown, then clarified: "confirmation from stakeholders is important and required. So demo is needed."
2. One proposal was refined and presented in full: demo and stakeholder confirmation in every mode. The stakeholder explicitly chose "Adopt proposal 1."
3. Framework designer verdict: no concern; one shared rule avoids mode-dependent acceptance, while direct demonstrations avoid unnecessary presentation work.
4. Framework guardian verdict: no concern provided the new decision explicitly replaces automatic user-driven achievement, preserves human confirmation and role-free solo mode, and does not imply roadmap or milestone approval. The accepted proposal incorporates these conditions.
5. Coordinator verdict: no concern; readiness, demonstration evidence, confirmation, and progression remain traceable in existing records without a new tracking file. No dissenting opinions were recorded.

## Decision

1. **Readiness:** In every collaboration and driving mode, meeting all observable milestone criteria makes the milestone ready for a demo, not achieved.
2. **Demo:** Deliver an evidence-backed demonstration. Run or directly show the outcome where possible; otherwise prepare and present HTML slides explaining the outcome and its acceptance evidence. Slides do not compensate for unmet criteria.
3. **Confirmation:** Achievement requires the demo followed by explicit confirmation from the project's designated human stakeholder acceptance authority. In solo mode, the user confirms without introducing roles. With several stakeholders, follow the project's accepted authority arrangements; unclear authority means confirmation cannot be inferred.
4. **Progression:** Until confirmation is received, keep the milestone unachieved and do not advance the current milestone on that basis. Independent work retains its existing permissions.
5. **Trace:** Record the demo date, evidence or artifact reference, and explicit confirmation in the existing roadmap progress log. Silence, checked criteria, AI judgement, or delivery of slides do not constitute confirmation.
6. **Follow-through:** Replace automatic achievement in user-driven mode while preserving the AI-driven safeguard. Align the glossary, the orchestrator's shared and mode-specific rules, the project-start roadmap template (including a Demo field and confirmation guidance), and affected overview understanding through reviewed implementation work.
7. **Boundaries:** This changes the framework rule only. It does not adopt the Q13 roadmap, confirm any milestone, reopen Q15, or authorize pushing or installation. Earlier decisions remain immutable. Installed-skill updates and external sharing retain their separate approved flows.

## Explanation

1. This explicitly supersedes criteria-only milestone achievement in user-driven mode under the earlier lifecycle framework. It preserves the AI-driven safeguard and resolves the remaining applicability question left in explanation point 4 of the AI-driven delegation decision. Prior records are not edited.
2. The demo and confirmation requirement applies equally to solo, team, and organization projects, and to user-driven and AI-driven work. Solo remains role-free; AI cannot supply the stakeholder's confirmation.
3. A multi-stakeholder project uses its existing accepted acceptance authority. This decision neither invents a quorum nor presumes every stakeholder has agreed.
4. The presentation form follows the nature of the outcome. HTML slides explain outcomes that cannot be directly demonstrated; they are not a substitute for acceptance evidence.
5. Requiring stakeholder availability and, where needed, an HTML presentation is an accepted trade-off for explicit acceptance.

5. Do not commit, push, install, or rewrite earlier decisions. Report that external sharing and installed-skill updates have not been performed.
