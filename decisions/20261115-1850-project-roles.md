# Project roles: per-project roster from a starter catalogue

**Accepted by:** Maxim Nikitin (project manager)
**Date:** 2026-11-15

## Problem Description

Q2 asked how roles, their expertise, and their known biases are defined, how an issue's required expertise is matched to roles, and how the stakeholder differs from contributors. `overview.md` already said that roles carry expertise and biases and that any role holder can be a human or an AI, but nothing defined roles per project, said when they take part, or made the skills use them.

Founder vision: a real project involves different interests, and each role represents one. Decisions should be challenged from those perspectives within the same session, to limit token use, and only where it makes sense. For example, a project manager stays out of technical decisions but joins when a decision affects the project side. Agreed risks: roles blurring into one voice, and role input becoming bureaucracy. A game-project pilot gave five roles one concrete condition each on a single decision.

Three proposals were considered: a per-project roster from a starter catalogue with roles joining on triggers; a fixed catalogue for every project; and per-project roles with no starter catalogue.

## Decision

The per-project roster from a starter catalogue, with roles joining on triggers, is accepted with the founder's refinements:

1. **Definition:** a role states its name, the interest it represents, its expertise, its triggers (the kinds of decisions it joins), its red lines, and its known bias.
2. **Catalogue and roster:** Project Guide ships a starter catalogue (`skills/glossary/roles-catalogue.md`). Each project keeps its roster in a Roles subsection of the overview's Collaboration section. Roles to add are proposed: from the catalogue, choosing only those the project needs, and defined from scratch with the same fields for any needed expertise the catalogue lacks, as in a new domain. The same applies whenever a later issue needs expertise that no role in the roster has. The roster may define new roles and may redefine catalogue roles; the roster's definition wins.
3. **Stakeholder:** a distinct role held by the user. It owns requirements, preferences, acceptance, and the reserved decisions, and is never played by the AI.
4. **Expertise tagging:** roles are tagged wherever their input or expertise is required. At registration or refinement, an issue lists the roles whose expertise it requires, and resolution points may do the same.
5. **Decision authority:** a decision on an issue that requires expertise is made only by holders of that expertise. The stakeholder keeps the reserved decisions. In solo mode this rule is relaxed: the one person decides every issue, and any role perspective counts as opinion.
6. **Expert input and opinions:** the required expertise owners review the proposals, and each gives a one- or two-line verdict: objection, condition, or no concern, flagged when it matches the role's known bias. All other input, from other roles or anyone else, is opinion. Opinions are recorded briefly and attributed, to show the complete picture and keep the think-tank space open, but they do not bind the decision.
7. **Holders:** any role can be held by a human or an AI. AI roles run as lenses within one session. Separate per-role agent sessions are a possible later improvement, adopted only if evidence shows the single session harms decisions.
8. **Conflicts:** a conflict between expertise owners is settled by the owner of the affected interest. In AI-driven mode, a conflict that touches a reserved decision goes to the stakeholder with both positions and a recommendation. In user-driven mode, the user settles every conflict.
9. **Skills:** `start-project` proposes roles to add; `raise-issue` and `refine` tag the required expertise; `refine` collects verdicts and records opinions; `decide` lets only expertise owners decide and records their objections and conditions; `complete-task` has expertise owners check the criteria in their area; `work-on-project` reads the roster. The glossary defines role, interest, roster, role catalogue, verdict, opinion, expert opinion, and stakeholder.

## Explanation

1. Projects carry only the roles they need, and roles cost nothing until a trigger fires, which keeps the process light.
2. Verdicts make each interest's challenge explicit, and bias flags keep each role's tilt visible.
3. A person working with AI role holders is team mode, as the [earlier AI-driven decision](20261004-1852-ai-driven-mode.md) already states for an AI team. Solo mode still uses no roles.
4. This applies the opinion and expert-opinion distinction in `overview.md` section 8 to roles: expertise decides whose input binds a decision.
5. The founder refined points 2 and 4 to 6 on the day of acceptance, before the decision was shared.
6. A feedback loop that returns roles defined in projects to the catalogue is left for later (Q24).

## Consequences

- Add the starter catalogue, and integrate roles into the skills, the glossary, and `overview.md`.
- Q2 is removed from `open-issues.md`.
- Projects that already pilot roles move their roles into the overview's Roles subsection.
- Q24 is registered for growing the catalogue from project rosters.
